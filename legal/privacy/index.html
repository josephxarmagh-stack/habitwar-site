Privacy Policy

Last updated October 17, 2026

Habit War is made and run by Nicholas Piva, an independent developer in Massachusetts, USA ("we", "us"). He decides how your data is used (the "data controller" under EU and UK law). This policy says what we collect, why, who else handles it, how long we keep it, and what you can do. The short version: no ads, ever. We never sell your data. Your Health and Oura numbers are seen only by you. 1. What we collect Your account • Your email address, and your password (stored only as a scrambled hash). With Sign in with Apple: the email Apple gives us (it can be a private relay address) and Apple's sign-in token, kept so we can disconnect Apple when you delete your account. • Username, display name, and what you add to your page: profile picture, bio, tags and up to six page photos, plus a town if you added one in an earlier version of the app. • Who invited you, if you joined through a friend's link. Your habits • Habit names, schedules, goals, sections, notes and check-ins (done, missed, rest day), and the streaks and scores made from them. • Proof photos you add to a check-in. Friends, wars and messages • Friend requests, friends, blocks, nudges, posts, comments and reactions. • Wars you start or join: name, habit, dates, the forfeit, results and forfeit photos. • Messages: direct messages with friends and the talk inside each war. • Reports you send, with a copy of what you reported. Apple Health, Oura and location (all optional): see sections 2, 3 and 4. Your device • A push notification token (only if you allow notifications), your time zone (so reminders come at the right hour), iPhone or web, and the app version. • Kept on your phone only: a copy of your habits and unsent changes so the app works offline, and a widget key plus today's habits so the Home Screen widget can check you in. How the app is used • Usage events: actions like opening the app, adding a habit or checking in, linked to your account and kept in our own database. Before you sign up, sign-up steps are counted with a random ID made on your device. • Crash reports: when the app hits an error, Sentry gets the error type, app version and device type — never your name, email, what you wrote, or Health data. • Your IP address: our servers and hosting providers see it with each request and use it for security and to stop abuse. 2. Apple Health (iPhone, optional) • Habit War reads Apple Health only after you allow it on Apple's own screen, and never writes to it. • Your phone compares the readings with your habit's goal. When you hit it, the habit checks itself off. The check-in syncs to your account and follows your sharing choice; the reading itself stays on your phone unless you turn on Save my daily totals. • Save my daily totals is off until you say yes. When it is on, each day's activity and sleep totals — steps, walking and cycling distance, workouts and workout minutes, exercise minutes, active calories, stand hours, flights climbed, time in daylight, water, mindful minutes, and sleep with its deep, REM and core stages — are stored with that day's check-in so you can see your history. Only you can see them. • Resting heart rate, heart rate variability, breathing rate, blood oxygen, VO2 max and weight are never sent to us or anyone else: Habit War keeps them only on your phone. • Health data is never used for advertising or marketing and never sold. Habit War never puts Health data in iCloud itself; the copy the app keeps on your phone is part of your phone's own backup (iCloud Backup or your computer), if you use one. Your Health numbers go to no one except the database provider that stores them for us (section 7); friends see only check-ins, under your sharing choice. • Turn totals off in Settings → Connections → Apple Health settings. To stop all reading, turn Habit War off in the Health app's settings. Turning totals off stops new ones; totals already saved are deleted with that check-in, the habit or your account. 3. Oura and other connected services (optional) • Oura: when you tap Sign in with Oura, Oura asks you to approve, and our server keeps the access key Oura gives us. Every 15 minutes our server asks Oura for your recent days and keeps only these numbers: sleep time and stages (deep, REM, light), sleep efficiency, time to fall asleep, bedtime, sleep, readiness and activity scores, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, body temperature change, blood oxygen, steps and active calories. • We use these numbers only to check off and show your ring tiles. They are stored on our server and only you can see them; friends see only a tile's done or missed check-in, under your sharing choice. • Disconnect any time in Settings → Connections: the key is erased at once and reading stops. Numbers already saved stay with your check-ins until you delete them, the habit or your account. • Shopify, Stripe, GitHub, Todoist and RescueTime connections are open only to accounts we invite. They work the same way: we keep the key you give us, read only the numbers your tiles need, and erase the key when you disconnect. • Oura and these services have their own privacy policies for the data they hold. 4. Location (optional) • My area, for area boards (coming soon): if you tap Use my location once in Edit Profile, your iPhone finds your rough position (to about a kilometer) one time and asks Apple to turn it into place names. Only the names of your country, state, county and town are saved, never your exact location. They stay private. Your coordinates are never saved or sent to us, and your area is never shown on your page. You can also pick it by hand or change it in Edit Profile. • Town on my page: earlier versions of the app let you show a town on your page. If you did, it still shows there to the people who can see your page. Email us and we remove it. 5. Who can see what • Anyone who opens your page link can see your username, display name, profile picture, bio and tags. • Who can see my habits (Settings) decides who sees your habits and check-ins: Only me, Friends, or Everyone. You can change a single habit in its Edit. • Your page and its photos are seen by your friends, or by anyone when you choose Everyone. With Everyone, your page is on the web at gethabitwar.com/<your username>, you appear in Search, search engines may list it, and shared links show a preview card. • War members see your check-ins for the war's habit, the war's talk and its forfeit photos. Anyone in the app can find a public war. • Messages are seen only by the people in the conversation, and by us if one is reported (with up to five messages before and after it). Direct messages are only possible between friends. • Your profile picture is stored at a public web address. Proof photos are private files shown through links that expire. • Only you see your email, your Health and Oura numbers, and your area. • Block anyone at any time: it ends sharing and messages both ways. 6. Why we use it • To run Habit War: your account, board, friends, wars, messages and notifications. • To check habits off from Apple Health and the services you connect. • To keep people safe: reviewing reports, removing content that breaks our Terms, and a filter that blocks the worst words in messages. • To fix bugs and see which features are used (our own usage events and crash reports). • To answer you when you write to us. No ads, ever. We never sell your data or share it for advertising. We don't track you across other apps or websites, so there is nothing for a browser's Do Not Track signal to turn off, and we don't respond to it. EU and UK legal bases: running the service you signed up for (contract); your consent for Apple Health totals, Oura and other connections, location and notifications; and our legitimate interest in safety, stopping abuse, and our own usage and crash statistics. 7. Who else handles your data These providers handle your data only to provide their service to us, and they protect it at least as well as this policy does: • Supabase — database, sign-in and file storage (United States). • Apple — Sign in with Apple, iPhone notifications, turning a location into place names, and the App Store. • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging — delivers notifications. • Resend — sends sign-in and password emails (it gets your email address). • Sentry — crash reports (United States). • GitHub Pages — hosts the website gethabitwar.com. • Oura — only if you connect your ring. Notifications pass through Apple and Google. They can include names (who checked in, who wrote to you), habit and war names, and — for messages — the words, unless the person receiving it has turned off Notification previews in Settings → Notifications. We never put your Health or Oura numbers in notifications. Your data is stored in the United States. If you use Habit War from another country, it is sent to and kept in the US. We may also share data when the law requires it (for example a valid court order) or to protect someone from serious harm. 8. How long we keep it • Your account data: until you delete your account (except the sign-in security log, below). • Direct messages: a conversation is deleted 30 days after its last message. If either person deletes their account, the conversation is deleted for both. • War talk: deleted 7 days after the war ends. The automatic war lines (like "Sam joined") and the results stay for the other members. A war you started stays for its other members, with one of them as the new owner. Talk from wars that ended more than a week before this timer started (October 2026) is kept until we clear it. • Reports: a report and its copy of the reported content are kept as a safety record, even after the reported person deletes their account. Reports you sent are deleted with your account. • Oura key: erased when you disconnect or delete your account. • Push token: removed when you sign out while your phone is online, and when you delete your account. • Usage events: deleted with your account. Sign-up step events stay, without your account. • Crash reports: Sentry deletes them within 90 days. • Sign-in security log: Supabase, which runs our sign-in, logs each sign-in, sign-out, password change and other account event with the email and IP address used. This log is not deleted with your account: it is kept as a security record until we clear it. • IP addresses: our sign-in records keep the address each of your devices last used, until you delete your account. The sign-in security log keeps addresses as said above. A counter that limits Oura sign-in attempts keeps an address, with no account attached, until we clear it. Our hosting providers also see addresses in their own logs, under their own privacy policies. • Backups: our routine database backups are deleted after about two weeks (the clean-up runs with the next backup that works). Three one-time copies of our old database, made while moving Habit War to new servers (September 24, October 6 and October 7, 2026), are kept on our own computer until we delete them. • Everyone pages: when you leave Everyone or delete your account, your page stops showing your habits at once; its web address and link title can stay until our next website update. • We keep a short record that an account deletion was asked for and finished. 9. Your choices and rights • Change most of it in the app: Edit Profile, Who can see my habits, Notifications and Connections. • Get a copy: Settings → Export & backup gives you your check-ins as a CSV file and a backup file of your habits, check-ins and proof photos. For everything else (profile, friends, messages, saved Health and Oura numbers), email us and we will send it within 30 days. • Delete: Settings → Account → Delete account removes your account and data as section 8 describes. If it can't finish, the app says so; try again or email us. • Take back consent any time: turn off Save my daily totals, disconnect Oura, change your area, or turn off notifications. This stops future use; it doesn't undo what was done before. • Depending on where you live (for example the EU, the UK, California, Washington and other US states), you can ask to see, correct, delete or get a copy of your data, and object to or limit some uses. Email us; we answer within 30 days and never treat you worse for asking. You can also complain to your local data protection authority. • California: we do not sell or share personal information, and we don't use sensitive personal information to profile you. 10. Consumer health data This is our Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy, for Washington, Nevada and other US states with health data laws. It is part of our Privacy Policy and is also published at gethabitwar.com/legal/health. What we collect • Apple Health daily totals, only if you turn on Save my daily totals. • Oura readings, only if you connect your Oura ring. • Check-ins of habits that track your health, and habit names or notes that say something about your health (for example a habit to take medicine). Where it comes from You, Apple Health on your iPhone, and Oura. Why we use it Only to check off and show your habits and your own history. Who gets it • Supabase, the database provider that stores it for us. • People who can see a habit (your choice in Who can see my habits, and the members of a war it is in): they see its name, its check-ins and any note or proof photo you add, never your Health or Oura numbers. • Apple and Google, which deliver notifications: a notification can name a habit and a streak, never your Health or Oura numbers. We never sell health data and never use it for ads or marketing. Your consent • Apple Health totals and Oura readings: only after you say yes — on Apple's Health permission screen, to the Save my daily totals question, and with Sign in with Oura. • Habit names, notes and check-ins: when you add them, to run the app you asked for. Your rights You can see your data, delete it, or take back your consent at any time: turn off Save my daily totals or disconnect Oura in Settings → Connections, delete a habit or your account, or email us at support@gethabitwar.com. We answer within 30 days. 11. Children Habit War is for people 13 and older. We don't knowingly collect data from children under 13; if we learn we have, we delete the account. Parents can write to us. 12. Security Data travels encrypted (TLS), and database rules let each person read only what they are allowed to see. Your phone keeps a copy of your habits so the app works offline, so keep your phone locked. No system is perfect: tell us about any problem at support@gethabitwar.com. 13. Changes When this policy changes, the date at the top changes. Big changes — a new kind of data, or new people who can see it — are also listed in the app's update notes ("What's New") on the App Store. Contact: Nicholas Piva · support@gethabitwar.com
